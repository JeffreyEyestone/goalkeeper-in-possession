The Goalkeeper in Possession: Visible Options, Retention Risk, and Asymmetric Turnover Cost

Introduction. Goalkeeper distribution is usually evaluated after the pass is played, yet coaches decide before it: build short, play through pressure, or go direct. We test whether the options visible to a goalkeeper are associated with those choices, whether they add information to retention modeling, and how that probability should be interpreted when failure near goal is disproportionately costly.

Methods. We analyze 45,974 goalkeeper distributions from 722 StatsBomb open-data matches across five elite cohorts. A target-free expected-retention model (xR-GK) is evaluated with true leave-one-competition-out testing and independent out-of-fold value models. We additionally analyze 4,831 goalkeeper distributions with StatsBomb 360 freeze frames from the 2022 World Cup and Euro 2024. Visible teammates are classified by distance, centrality, receiver pressure, and whether a 1.5-m passing corridor is clear. On 4,802 exact joins to frozen xR-GK, five-fold match-grouped models test whether two 360 variables - clear short-option count and a visible central short exit - add information beyond locally recalibrated xR, event pressure, goal-kick state, and competition.

Results. Direct play was observed on 85.9% and 91.9% of distributions with no clear short exit in the World Cup and Euro samples, respectively, falling to 27.2% and 25.8% with two or more clear short exits; the pattern survived completed-pass and lane-width sensitivities. Adding the two 360 variables improved match-held-out AUC from 0.648 to 0.699 (delta=0.051, 95% CI 0.035-0.066), Brier score by 0.0065, and log loss by 0.0167, with the same direction in both tournaments and after visible-area control. Event-only xR retained moderate discrimination in unseen competitions (AUC 0.645-0.710) but absolute probabilities shifted materially. Independently refit cohort models also showed that opponent consequence after failure exceeded the origin possession value in every environment (C/V 1.40-1.90), making the complete lost branch 2.40-2.90 times origin value.

Conclusion. Goalkeeper distribution is an option-constrained decision under asymmetric risk. In two tournaments, visible short-option structure is strongly associated with observed short-versus-direct choice and adds held-out retention information beyond event-only controls, while probability scale remains environment-dependent and failure near goal is systematically costly. For clubs, the framework replaces a universal “good with his feet” score with an opponent- and game-model-specific decision profile for build-up design, recruitment, opposition pressing, and goalkeeper training.
